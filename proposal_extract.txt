Introduction, Background   The accelerated evolution of the deep learning and video vision has tremendously enhanced the potential of automatic video cognition in many fields of application, including self-driving cars, medical diagnosis, community safety, and sports statistics. Sports analytics is one of these areas that have emerged as an influential field of research because of its essential contribution concerning the decision-making process of coaches, as well as training efficiency and better viewing experience among spectators. There has been a growing usage of data-driven insights in modern sports organisations, and automated, scalable and correct analysis pipelines which can handle large amounts of video data with little human intervention are urgently needed. Basketball is also one of the games that pose extremely difficult conditions on analytical systems based on vision. Basketball is not a task that is either used with isolated objects or with simple trajectories as compared to the case of multiple players who move rapidly on a limited area, multiple occlusions and interactions among players, referees and the ball are also complex. The rapid transformation of the formation, the fluctuation of camera perspective and the uneven light cause even more difficulties in determining what is actually there in a visual form. The former methods of basketball analysis typically relied on manual or hand-based annotation, subjective annotation, or composite heuristics that are not very scalable, lack objectivity and require significant labor. Even though there have been recent advances in detecting objects, tracking and even pose estimation, recent studies have still some shortcomings. The deployed pipelines are not connected, as they do not provide the end-to-end form with detection, tracking, and classification of the teams, as well as pose estimation. Jersey based team assignment is inapplicable in real world conditions owing to occlusion, low resolutions and similarities in color. Additionally, a great number of the scholarly solutions fail to fulfill the criteria of the real-time evaluation or neglect some practical factors including the ethical processing of the athlete data, business viability, or durability of the system. These issues emphasize the need to have a holistic and contemporary solution that would incorporate state-of-the-art vision models into a harmonized pipeline to be used in basketball. Newer models like YOLOv11 and RTMPose have demonstrated a significant reduction in error as well as inference rate, and consumer-level hardware (e.g., RTX 30-series GPUs) has become sufficiently fast to run computationally expensive models. Simultaneously, the growing focus on the responsible AI practices renders it essential that such systems should consider the issues of data privacy, equity, and harmless use. Sports analytics based on artificial intelligence is also sustainable, as it will minimize recurring manual annotation and enhance the efficiency of operations in the long term. Professionally, the development of a strong and morally sound basketball analysis system indicates the objectives of modern industry trends in intelligent sports technology and, therefore, it is technologically as well as socially applicable. Driven by these needs, this paper presents a single pipeline of basketball video analysis the framework would integrate detention, tracking, filtering, team classification, and pose estimation into a single pipeline. By incorporating state-of-the-art detection (YOLOv11), identity-preserving tracking (DeepSORT), team classification through clustering (with correction) as well as a high-precision pose estimation (RTMPose), it is expected that the proposed system will provide a robust and scalable real-world basketball analytics system . Scope and Objective s The current project is aimed at the design and implementation of an end-to-end basketball video analysis system which may detect and track players, referees, and the ball; filter irrelevant detections; classify players as team players; estimate poses of players and project their positions on a 2D tactical plane. The pipeline delivers framewise structured data that is used to aid downstream tactical analysis and performance analysis . Multi-camera 3D reconstruction, model-based ball physics, high-level action recognition (e.g. shot prediction or play classification), audio analysis or mobile deployment optimization are all outside of scope. Omitting these elements will assure that the project is technically focused and is within the available resources, as well as, a defined boundary of the functionality of the system . The measurable objectives targeted in the project are : Create a unified detecting module that is powered by YOLOv11 and can recognize players, referees, and the ball, in basketball video footage . Deploy a powerful tracking system with DeepSORT to ensure that there is a stable player ID and the aim is to have fewer IDs changed and less player track fragmentation . Propose appearance-based team classification model through clustering and semi-automated correction to get feasible grouping of players into two teams when in real life situation . Combine with RTMPose to do full-body pose estimation, which allows for the extraction of 17 keypoints per player and high spatial accuracy . Outputs: generate frame-level output with structured data stored in the JSON format, including the bounding boxes, identities, team labels and pose data . Produce a 2D tactical map with a reasonable level of localization accuracy of the position of the players on the court . These goals make the system technically coherent, practically verifiable and according to modern evaluation standards in sports analytics with the help of computer vision . Literature Review Deep Learning-based Approach Basketball video analysis has numerous applications in deep learning, in particular, in object detection, action recognition, and behavior analysis. Deep learning methods, based on the application of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and other architectures of deep neural networks, are capable of automatically isolating significant features in large volumes of video records, particularly in complex and dynamic game sequences of basketball: high-speed player movement, and frequent occlusions. As an example, Wang et al. (2024) have embraced the concept of deep learning alongside the examination of spatiotemporal features to effectively achieve the task of identifying player actions in basketball matches, which is enhanced drastically in regard to precision and can be utilized effectively in areas of tactical choices [1]. On the same note, Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2024) were able to develop a three-stage system that utilizes deep learning to process basketball video, greatly increasing the ability to process data in real-time, and automatically extract game data based on broadcasts videos as well [2]. Although deep learning has serious benefits in terms of action recognition and player tracking, it still demands high processor power and large volumes of training data, so it is more applicable in those environments that have ample resources and extensive data. In situations where resources are constrained, it can be required to look elsewhere to ones having fewer computational demands. Tracking-based Approach Continuous tracking of players and basketballs in basketball video is mostly done using tracking-based methods. Such techniques are based on object detection and position prediction and typical algorithms are Kalman filtering and particle filtering and more recent models such as YOLO. These algorithms operate the sequential video frames and forecast and monitor a location variation of targets. Fu et al. (2020) suggested a better multi-object tracking (MOT) algorithm that was effective in tackling the problem of occlusion and crowding in basketball by integrating both Kalman filter and particle filter [4]. The tracking-based algorithms by Rodriguez-Garcia et al. (2024) are also applied to improve player tracking in basketball videos, thus adding the deep learning techniques [2] to the existing image tracking. Tracking-based methods usually use fewer computational resources compared with deep learning methods, and so can be used in real-time. Nevertheless, these modes can be faced with issues as loss tracking or ID change in tricky situations, notably in the processing of game videos on a large scale. Irrespective of these limitations, tracking-based algorithms have still great potential in real-time video processing and resource-constrained settings. Fusion and Dataset Construction Approach of multiple features Multi-feature fusion technique gives the power to recognise more features in the video like colour, texture and posture through a combination of different forms of information. Such techniques can also be applied especially well to complex basketball video cases where it can successfully cope with occlusions, high-speed player actions, and other difficulties. Instead, dataset construction is more concerned with offering annotated datasets of high quality to facilitate training and validation of the said methods. The research of Pan et al. Through using multi-feature fusion methods, they were able to enhance the accuracy of basketball skill assessment particularly in multi-featured scenarios [6]. On the same note, He and others (2024) suggested a posture-guided feature alignment algorithm, which combined several visual features to enhance player detection and recognition in a basketball situation [7]. The strength of this strategy is that it will unify different sources of information which increases the flexibility of the system to complicated situations. In addition, data creation has offered fundamental backup to the development of such approaches and this greatly enhanced the model notebook functions in intricate scenarios and protracted track activities. Nevertheless, multi-feature fusion and dataset construction are greatly dependent on good data and the means of computations, which are yet bottlenecks in some areas. Project Plan Proposed Solution / Methodology The suggested basketball video analysis system is a modular pipeline architecture since basketball scenes have a number of unique problems that cannot be resolved with only one monolith model. To begin with, there is a demand of the spatial detection of players and the time tracking because high-speed movement of players and frequent occlusions require separate approaches. Second, the color based features that are used in team identification are distinctly different than motion features and shape features. Third, the quality of pose estimation with tracked and filtered detections is that it necessitates fewer computations and higher keypoint accuracy by concentrating on verified human beings. The work is divided into specialized modules: detection, tracking, classification and pose estimation; therefore, one element can be optimized with independent components, and end-to-end differentiability is provided where needed . The general system structure is that of a modular but tightly coupled pipeline as shown in Figure 3.1. The videos of the raw basketball matches are initially analyzed using the object detection module based on the YoLOv11 to obtain the bounding boxes of players, referees, and the basketball. This is then sent to DeepSORT tracking module which tracks identities across frames in a consistent manner through Kalman filter and appearance re-identification. To isolate tracks of players, a filtering stage is used to eliminate non-player objects (referees and balls). The second step involves Labelling the players to their respective teams through the grouping of HSV color histograms by KMeans algorithm which is then classified into the team classification module. The bounding boxes of the filtered player are then processed into the RTMPose component that will yield 17-joint skeletal keypoints per player. Lastly, a 2D court projection module converts image coordinates into image positions to standard court coordinates to allow visualization of tactics and spatial analysis. This design has multi-stage design that makes sure that every module gets clean and contextually suitable inputs which increases the overall system robustness and accuracy . Figure 3.1: System Architecture Diagram YOLOv11 Detection Module YOLOv11 is able to cause a single forward pass to do detection, unlike two-stage detectors like Faster R-CNN, meaning that inference time is much less and again, it comes with competitive mean Average Precision (mAP) performance. Additionally, YOLOv11 has a superior architectural development (e.g., C2f units) that maximize feature extractions by efficient cross-stage partial connections and a decoupled architecture that divides the classifier and localization functions to provide an enhanced gradient flow. The above properties render YOLOv11 especially useful in multiclassification of objects (players, referees, balls) in crowded and dynamic basketball environments where speed and accuracy are both significant . Considering an input RGB image I, YOLOv11 splits it into a grid and predicts bounding box positions, objectness and class probabilities of each grid square. The backbone network (based on CSPNet-inspired designs) extracts multi-scale features and are further sent through a neck module (a network that is commonly a feature pyramid) to distribute information among the different scales. A detection head produces a bounding box (center x, center y, width, height), an objectness confidence score that represents the probability of an object actually being present, and each object category as a probability in it being one of the targets (player, referee, ball). The composite loss function is used to train the model where the localization accuracy and classification correctness and objectness prediction are balanced. The total loss is defined as : L total  = λ box ·L box  + λ cls ·L cls  + λ obj ·L obj      (3.1) where: L box : Bounding box regression loss (CIoU Loss) L cls : Classification loss (Binary Cross-Entropy) L obj : Objectness confidence loss λ: Weight hyperparameters The CIoU (Complete IoU) loss is specifically defined as: L CIoU  = 1 - IoU + ρ²(b, b gt )/c² + αv     (3.2) The YOLOv11 object detector module gives a list of bounding boxes containing every object of each frame, each containing: (1) the spatial coordinates (x, y, w, h) of the object and size, (2) a confidence score of how well the object is detected, and (3) a category label (player, referee, or ball). The downstream modules are based on these detections. The strategies in implementing the tracking, classification, and pose estimation steps are made of high-quality detections with localization accuracy and low false positive rates to make sure that the errors during the pipeline transmission are minimal . DeepSORT Tracking Module DeepSORT is executed in two steps, prediction and update. During the prediction phase a Kalman filter tracks a state (position and velocity) of each tracked object and models the state of the tracked object within the following frame, using a linear model of its motion. State vector  x t  comprises of bounding box center positions, aspect ratio, and respective velocities. The forecasting stage is modeled as follows : x t  = F·x t-1  + w t      (3.3) F state transition matrix wt process noise. The prediction model takes advantage of the measured detection to the predicted state : z t  = H·x t  + v t      (3.4) H is observation matrix and vt noise in measurement. Total matching cost  : c(i,j) = λ m ·d m (i,j) + (1-λ m )·d a (i,j)     (3.5) DeepSORT tracking module gives every identified player a unique and permanent identity (track ID) throughout the video stream. Output per frame contains: (1) object bounding box coordinates of each tracked object, (2) a unique track identifier that stays steady as the player navigates the scene and (3) a confidence score which gives information on the reliability of tracking. Such temporal consistency is vital in the downstream jobs like team classification, pose estimation and trajectory analysis. Through constant identities, DeepSORT also allows the system to track information through time, calculate player specific statistics and create consistent visualizations to track individual players through the game . Team Classification Module The team classification makes use of the KMeans clustering algorithm to the HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) color histograms, which are taken out of the entire player bounding boxes. The HSV colour space (which out of the three colour spaces), is preferred over RGB since it has a greater ability to dissociate the colour (hue) and the brightness (value) therefore the technique is more effective when dealing with colored images in sports videos, where lighting changes are often present. Each tracked player bounding box is transformed into a 3D Hsp histogram Hi using the hue, saturation, and value channels as bins and finding the number of times each combination of color appears in the player region. The next step is the normalization of these histograms so that the resultant feature vectors reflect the prevalent color distribution of uniforms of each player: minimize the within cluster sum of squared distances : J = Σ k=1 2  Σ i∈Ck  ||H i  - μ k ||²     (3.6) where  H i    is the histogram of player  i , Ck is the group of players, which are distributed to cluster  μ k  and muk is the centroid of the cluster k. Once converged, the different clusters are then denoted as teams and the players are identified as such. A filtering step is used to eliminate low-confidence classifications or outliers, not to take into account edge cases like referees or accounts of misclassified players, using similarity thresholds of histograms. Temporal consistency is also imposed by using majority voting an a sliding window of frames such that the label of a player on a team does not change in an erratic manner because of temporary color variations or occlusions. . RTMPose Pose Estimation Module Bounding box detection and tracking give details on the location and identity of players, but fail to give a detailed view on a bodily posture or limb position. The pose of players is a key aspect in higher-level applications in action recognition (e.g. shooting, dribbling, defending), biomechanical analysis, injury prevention and coaching feedback .  The model is fed an image region centered on the bounding box of a player and gives 2D locations of 17 body keypoints such as head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, and ankles in the COCO keypoint format as output. RTMPose uses a transformer-based design which learns geometrical associations among keypoints by the use of self-attention processes, such that it can better handle occlusions and partial views compared with conventional CNN-based models . The RTMPose module is a fine-pose pose estimator that predicts 17 body keys of each identified player. Its wide use in models like action recognition, biomechanics, and pose-based performances, which cannot be simply considered position tracking, makes this transformer-based model a better and more precise choice . (mAP) is the measure the industry uses since it considers precision (falses) and recall (finding all the true objects) through the various confidence levels. To track it, Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) is used since it is both false negatives (not found), false positives (incidental tracks), and identity switches (ID swaps), which are essential to the preservation of consistent identities of players. PN03 Pose estimation is performed using Percentage of Correct Keypoints (PCK), as the accuracy metric is intuitive with respect to the spatial accuracy of predicted keypoints as they are regarded to lie withing a predefined distance threshold that is similar to ground-truth annotations . Detection Evaluation:  The mean of all the values of Average Precision (AP) over all the classes of objects (player, referee, ball) creates the Mean Average Precision (mAP). The formula is : mAP = (1/C) · Σ c=1 C  AP c      (3.7) In which C denotes the number of classes (C=3) and AP c denotes the average precision of class c. A detection is said to be right when Intersection of Union (IoU) of a detection with a ground truth box is more than 0.5 or 0.75 (depending on the evaluation strictness). An increase in mAP values will mean improved performance on localization and classification . Tracking Evaluation:  Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) is an all-inclusive measure where errors in tracking are punished such as false negatives (FN), false positives (FP), and identity switch (IDSW). It is defined as : MOTA = 1 - (FN + FP + IDSW) / GT     (3.8) where GT is the overall number of ground truth objects in all the frames. MOTA is in the negative infinity to 1 scale as a high value implies that the tracking performance is good. Strong performance in the multi-object tracking benchmarks is usually determined as a MOTA of above 0.8 (80%) . Pose Estimation Evaluation:  Percentage of Correct Keypoints (PCK) is the percentage of predicted keypoints which are within a given distance threshold (`alpha`) of their ground truth locations. The threshold usually is a fraction of a reference length dref, e.g. head size or torso height. PCK is computed as : PCK@α = (1/N) · Σ i=1 N  [d i  &lt; α · d ref ]     (3.9) and N is the count of keypoints,  d i  is the Euclidean distance between the forecasted and actual position of keypoint i and [ ] is the indicator function . Expected Results YOLOv11 has been shown to achieve mAP over 85 per cent on general object detection tasks like COCO, and we expect similar or even worse performance on basketball videos because of the high density of players, regular occlusions and motion blur. DeepSORT has reported MOTA scores of over 75 percent on pedestrian tracking data, and we believe that it will perform equally or better due to the comparatively limited sets of motion on a basketball court. The KMeans classification of teams based on color histograms is resistant to changes in lighting when HSV color space is used and we expect that classification will be above 90% accurate based on the fact that team uniforms are distinct and noticeable to the eye in most professional matches. Radiohead RTMPose has shown values of PCKx0.5 exceeding 85% across the COCO keypoint benchmarks, and we anticipate slightly lower performance because of partial occlusions, as well as non-frontal viewpoints that occur frequently during basketball shots . Qualitative Results Qualitative results are expected to demonstrate stable detection and tracking of all the players over long sequences, where the bounding boxes are tight around the player’s body and the track IDs of the players are consistent over time, unless there is an occlusion or change in camera angle. Team classification should result in two clearly separable regions of visualised players from Team A (e.g. blue) and Team B (e.g. red), and errors should be rare, and limited to frames with challenging lighting conditions or unusual player uniform colours. Pose estimation outputs should display accurate skeletons over the overlayed mask, and keypoints should be in good agreement with the underlying anatomy, such that correctness can be visually verified. The 2D tactical map should provide a simple bird-eye view of player positions, where trajectories make intuitive sense and follow actual player paths, and team formations are clearly distinguishable. Sample output frames will be included in the final report to demonstrate qualitative outcomes . Progress Analysis  Current Progress Status To date, the current reporting period, a number of fundamental modules were already implemented, integrated and tested preliminarily. The YOLOv11 detector has now been trained using the annotated basketball dataset and is now functional, reaching reasonable performance on unseen test frames with respect to the detection task. The DeepSORT tracking module has already been included with the detector and can hold the identities of players through brief video clips, but additional fine-tuning is required in order to minimize the number of identity transitions during long occlusions. The module to break players into two teams by using KMeans clustering grouping HSV histograms has been done and results are encouraging, as in most of the test cases, the members were classified into the two teams. Previous MMPose weights were used to train RTMPose and extract pose keypoints are used to detect players that are filtered. The 2D court projection reasoning has been manually implemented with manually defined homography matrices, and some initial tactical displays have been created . The pose estimation component must be trained on basketball-specific data to increase the performance of the keypoint under non-normal viewpoints and occlusions. The algorithm of classifying the team needs some post-processing to occur to address the edge cases like referees, coach uniforms and players that had unusual uniform designs. The 2D projection module should be strengthened by adding automatic court line detection to it and homography estimation, which will make the module less dependent on manual annotation. Also, full-length match videotape end-to-end testing is not yet finished and quantitative measures of evaluation are still under collation. Conclusion   This paper has created an all-in-one basketball video analysis piping system that combines YOLOv11 detectors, DeepSORT trackers, team classification with KMeans, and MMPose pose estimation. The system has proven to be dependable in real game scenarios, with a high performance of accurate detection, constant identity tracking, and pose keypoint quality. Automation of long-term labor-intensive processes ensures pure practical utility of the framework in the field of sports analytics to increase the objectivity and efficiency of perception of players actions and team relationships . This is pioneering work because it has been able to incorporate several state-of-the-art computer vision modules into one consistent pipeline that suits basketball. The semi-automatic team classification system is more robust with realistic occlusions and lighting and the unified design eliminates the fragmentation of the current systems. The system has proven to be technically viable, running quickly on consumer level hardware and based on open-source and well-supported models, is applicable to both scholarly research and actual implementation . In the future, the next phase of the graduation project will evolve the system by adding additional cameras and 3D reconstruction, ball tracking, and more advanced innovation like play detection and tactical pattern analysis.
